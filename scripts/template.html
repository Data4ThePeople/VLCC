What the number means. Brokers quote tanker freight as a percentage of a yearly base rate. The Baltic Exchange and Fearnleys turn that into what the ship earns per day after fuel and port costs, spread over a round trip. That is the figure here. Two publishers are shown because neither covers the whole period alone; where they overlap, they agree within a few thousand dollars a day.

Since March 2026 the Baltic figure is an assessment for a voyage few ships are making, because the route loads inside the Strait of Hormuz. It still sets the price of freight contracts and futures, which is why it is the number the market quotes.
